You are building a padel player matchmaking quiz feature for a Flutter app. Create a six-question personality and goal assessment that categorizes players into archetypes, then integrate it with skill-based matching.
Quiz implementation: Build a six-question screen in Flutter with the following questions in Russian. Each question has three to four answer options. After all six questions, calculate the player archetype based on their answers.
Question one, after you lose a match, do you want to analyze what went wrong and improve, or do you want to laugh it off and move to the next game?
Question two, when you're on court, are you thinking about winning or about the energy and vibe with your partner?
Question three, do you play to push yourself hard and test your limits, or do you play mainly to have fun and disconnect from work stress?
Question four, do you prefer playing with people you already know and trust, or are you open to meeting new players and building new connections on court?
Question five, do you want to become a professional padel player and improve to your maximum potential, or is padel entertainment and hobby for you?
Question six, do you prefer to warm up for ten minutes before matches, or do you like to jump straight into playing?
Archetype clustering: Based on answers, assign players to one of five archetypes. Competitive improver: answers suggest analyzing losses, focused on winning, pushing limits, open to new players, pro-ambitious, prefers warm-up. Social enjoyer: answers suggest laughing off losses, focused on vibe, playing for fun, prefers trusted partners, treats padel as hobby, flexible on warm-up. Pro-ambitious: wants to become professional, analyzes losses, pushes limits, focused on winning, prefers warm-up. Casual relaxer: plays for fun, laughs off losses, focuses on vibe, hobby mindset, jumps straight into play. Connector: open to new players, values vibe and team energy, but not necessarily pro-focused, warm-up preference varies.
Data storage: Store the player's archetype string alongside their one-to-ten skill rating in the database. Also store warm-up preference as a boolean. Example: player name, skill level five, archetype social enjoyer, warm-up preferred true.
Matchmaking algorithm integration: When a player opens the match request page, query the database for available players. First priority: find players with the same archetype and skill level within plus or minus two points. Second priority: if no archetype match exists, find players with skill level within plus or minus three points regardless of archetype. Return the top three matches ranked by closest skill match. Display matched player profile with their skill level, archetype, and warm-up preference.
Post-match billing rule: After a match is completed, calculate payment splits based on court cost divided equally among four players. If a player arrives more than five minutes late, add a lateness penalty of one percent of court cost per minute late. Example: court costs four hundred. Each player pays one hundred. If a player is ten minutes late, they pay one hundred plus ten percent extra, equaling one hundred thirty. Other three players each pay ninety-seven point fifty. This is calculated and shown to players before payment confirmation.
Warm-up protocol note: Warm-up for ten minutes before matches is coach-recommended but optional. Display this as a suggestion when players match, not a requirement. Players can choose to skip it without penalty.
UI considerations: Show the matched player's archetype name and warm-up preference so both players know what to expect. Example: "You matched with Igor, level six, social enjoyer, prefers warm-up—you both value team vibe and proper preparation." Add a confirmation button where both players agree before the match is locked in.
Error handling: If no matches exist within skill range, show a message asking the player to check back soon or adjust their availability window.
